Can Dogs Eat Plums? Sweet Flesh Yes — the Pit Is Never Safe

By HappyPupGuide

Yes — a small piece of ripe plum flesh is safe for most dogs. Plums are juicy and sweet, and a few small pieces make a pleasant occasional treat. The rule that matters is simple: flesh only, pit always removed. The pit is the dangerous part, and it must never be offered to a dog.

The plum pit is a choking hazard, especially for small dogs, and it can block the gut if swallowed whole. Like other stone-fruit pits, it also contains amygdalin, a compound that can release cyanide when the pit is chewed or digested. A dog that crunches or swallows a pit should be watched closely, and a vet should be called if the dog seems unwell. Never offer a plum whole — always cut the fruit open, take the pit out, and throw it away where the dog cannot reach it.

Serve only fresh, ripe plums. Wash the fruit first, remove the pit, and cut the flesh into small pieces that suit the size of the dog. One or two small pieces are enough for a medium dog, and less for a small dog. Do not offer dried plums, or prunes: they are much more concentrated in natural sugars and can have a strong laxative effect, so they are not a good treat for dogs. Canned plums in syrup are also unsuitable, because of the added sugar.

Plums are a treat, not a meal. Treats of any kind should stay a small part of the day's food, with the dog's regular balanced diet doing the main work. Dogs with sensitive stomachs should only try new foods after a vet's okay, and any new food can cause soft stools or gas the first time. If you notice vomiting or diarrhoea, stop and go back to plain food for a day, and speak to your vet if it does not settle.
